## 5. The two scientific findings that change what the paper says

**`reach`'s constant-count gate crippled the real corpus.** `compatible()`
required equal constant counts, so **1,146 of Corpus B's 1,400 member pairs
(81.9 %) sat at infinite reach** and the coverage consensus was never given a
candidate set. That is what demoted an entire real-data corpus to the
supplement. Under `reach′` the figure is 387 (27.6 %), and on the worst run the
consensus goes from covering 13 of 35 members to **34 of 35**. The old
explanation — "a natural series is not a set of variants of one source, so there
is nothing for a consensus to converge to" — is **refuted** and must be
retracted in print. The corpus still goes to the supplement, because the
pre-registered gate fires on a null, but the reason is different and the gate is
badly underpowered (MDE 0.40 fact-tokens against observed differences of
0.03–0.11; on the recovery axis only 8 of 63 pairs are informative).

**H3a's deletion clause fails, and the boundary is not an artefact.** Measured on
in-domain deletion cells for the first time: `ins` 0.995 / 1.000 and `mix`
0.921 / 0.806 pass; `del` 0.845 / **0.276** fails. So the deletion boundary
survives `reach′` and is not caused by stranded constants either. **H3c** also
marginally fails: `majority_merge` recovery 0.894 against the consensus's 0.835
is 5.9 points against a pre-registered 5.
