### Assumptions (no question was asked)

1. **Generator.** No `HypergraphDataset` emits connected 3-uniform hypergraphs at a
   fixed `(n,m)`. I used the D-CONN1 primitive
   `isalhg.datasets.synthetic._random_hg.random_connected_hypergraph(arity_range=(3,3))`
   (rejection sampling with a backbone fallback), then asserted every centre has
   exactly `(n,m)`, is connected, and has no duplicate hyperedge.
2. **Edit operator.** `isalhg.core.sparse_hypergraph.random_connected_edit(H, rng, max_arity=3)`
   — the connectivity-guaranteeing wrapper over the Qin op set
   (`insert_vertex_and_edge`, `insert_hyperedge`, `add_incidence`,
   `delete_hyperedge`, `remove_incidence`). It does **not** include the incidence
   swap (`random_swap_edit` has no connectivity guarantee), so `swap` is absent
   here. Edits break 3-uniformity (arity-2 edges appear); this is inherent to the
   op set and stays inside `Σ_HG(3)`. Copies re-drawn if invalid.
3. **Distance path.** `d_I` is computed from `canonical_string(H, k=3)` → `parse` →
   `rapidfuzz.Levenshtein` under one process-wide token→code-point bijection, with
   memoization. This is the `isalhg_levenshtein` recipe with a cache; it was
   **asserted equal to `IsalHGLevenshtein(k=3).matrix(corpus)` on all 24 profiles**
   (`np.allclose`, no failures).
4. **Timeout.** A hard 30 s preemption cannot interrupt the C++ canonicalization
   backend, so the ceiling was enforced as a measured post-hoc check. Max observed
   0.026 s — four orders of magnitude of headroom; the guard never mattered.
5. **Search B semantics.** `current` is the ambient (generally non-canonical)
   string, per idea3 §3.2; the objective is always evaluated on the
   re-canonicalized decode `w*_c(S2H(s))`.

## 3. Reading

**(i) Does the medoid sit near the 2× bound?** No — it sits well inside it, and the
premise behind the question does not hold in this design. Certified against the
pairwise lower bound, `C_med / LB ≤ 1.63` on **every** profile (mean 1.507), so the
medoid is at worst a 1.63-approximation here, not a 2-approximation. More
importantly, **`C_med` is not ≈ 2·`C_0`; it is 0.87·`C_0`, and the medoid beats the
planted centre on 22 of 24 profiles.** The prediction `C_med ≈ 2·C_0` presumes
`M0` is (near) the optimum, which requires `d_I` to behave like the edit metric the
noise was applied in. It does not: under `d_I` the planted centre is an *outlier*
of its own cloud, roughly 11–19 tokens from each copy, while a copy sits ~10 tokens
from the others. `C_0` is therefore not a usable proxy for `OPT`, and any
experiment that reports "approximation ratio vs. the planted centre" will report a
number below 1 and mean nothing by it. Use `LB`.

**(ii) Does either search improve on the medoid?** Marginally, and it never
recovers `M0`. Search A improved on 9/24 profiles by a mean of 0.5–1.0 tokens;
Search B on 9/24 by 0–1.8 tokens. Against objectives of 56–130 tokens that is
**≤ 1.6 %**, and it costs 40–120 canonicalization-backed evaluations. Both searches
died at a local minimum after a mean of 0.4 improving steps; the 200-evaluation
budget was never exhausted, so a larger budget would not have helped. `M0` was
recovered once in 24 profiles by Search B, on the one profile where the medoid was
already at `d_I = 1` from `M0`. **On this evidence the generalized-median search is
not worth its cost, and idea3 §6 rule 4 fires: report the medoid as the output and
compress the search to a negative paragraph.** The one thing that did work
perfectly is the mechanism the paper claims is unique: every one of 24,000 ambient
single-token mutations decoded to a valid connected hypergraph, with no
reconstruction step and no validity filter.

**(iii) Is the landscape flat?** No — it is the opposite pathology, and that is
worse for local search. A single structural edit of the medoid changes the
objective by a **median of +21 to +38 tokens**, i.e. 25–35 % of `C_med`, with an
interquartile range of 12–47. The objective is steeply graded, but it grades
*uphill in essentially every direction*: only 0.6–0.9 % of structural neighbours
improve at all, and the best `ΔC` found in a near-exhaustive neighbourhood sweep is
0 to −3 tokens. The mechanism is visible per member: one edit moves the distance to
a given `K_i` by 3.5–5.9 tokens — roughly 40–60 % of the ~10-token distance being
optimized — and only 13–19 % of members get closer, with just 1.2–3.7 % of moves
bringing a majority of the seven closer. So the avalanche does not flatten the
landscape; it **destroys its resolution**. The smallest available move is comparable
in size to the whole quantity being minimized, so the medoid is a deep, isolated
local minimum whose basin has width zero, and no move operator built from single
structural or single-token edits can refine it. A generalized median under `d_I`
would need either a move set that is sub-avalanche (none is known — the avalanche is
a property of the canonical form, not of the operator) or a global method that does
not rely on local descent.

**Negative-result summary for the ledger.** Medoid: cheap, certified ≤ 1.63×`OPT`
here, and better than the planted centre. Generalized median by local search:
≤ 1.6 % gain, 0/24 recovery, dead on arrival because the objective's minimum
resolution is ~1/3 of the string. Decodability (P1): 24,000/24,000, the one claim
that survives intact.

## §2 Bipartite (Hungarian) GED metricity probe

### Report: is bipartite (Hungarian) GED a metric? — numerical verification

Script: `probe_bpged_metric.py` (same directory). Seed `20260903`, one
`random.Random` instance drives every draw — fully reproducible. Environment:
`~/.conda/envs/isalhg/bin/python` (numpy + scipy only). Raw counts:
`probe_bpged_metric_results.json`.

## Cost model (unit costs, undirected, vertex labels in {a, b}, unlabelled edges)

**Exact GED.** A candidate correspondence is a partial injective map
`phi: S1 -> V2` (`S1 ⊆ V1`); `V1\S1` is deleted, `V2\phi(S1)` is inserted.
Cost = Σ vertex substitutions (0 if labels match, else 1) + |deleted| +
|inserted| + edge cost, where the edge cost compares, for every unordered
pair `{i,j}` in `V1`, the presence of `{i,j}` in `E1` against the presence
of the corresponding pair in `E2` (via `phi`, when both endpoints survive;
1 if a present `E1` edge cannot survive because an endpoint is deleted),
plus every unordered pair in `V2` touching an inserted vertex whose edge is
present in `E2` (pairs already covered above are not double-counted). Exact
GED = min over all partial injective maps, brute-forced for `n ≤ 5`
(≤ 1546 candidate maps for `n1=n2=5`).

**Bipartite GED (Riesen & Bunke, *Image and Vision Computing* 27(7), 2009).**
`(n1+n2)×(n1+n2)` cost matrix: top-left `n1×n2` block
`c(u_i→v_j) = label_mismatch + |deg(u_i) − deg(v_j)|` (the `|Δdeg|` term is
the closed-form optimal local matching of the two incident-edge multisets
since edges carry no labels: `min(deg_i,deg_j)` edges match free, the excess
costs 1 each); top-right `n1×n1` diagonal `1+deg(u_i)` (deletion), off-diag
`BIG`; bottom-left `n2×n2` diagonal `1+deg(v_j)` (insertion), off-diag
`BIG`; bottom-right `n2×n1` all zero (ε→ε). Solved with
`scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment`. **Raw value** = sum of the selected
matrix entries. **Induced `d_BP`** = the real-to-real node correspondence
read off the assignment, replayed through the *exact* edit-path cost
function above — the actual cost of the implied edit path, i.e. the
quantity conventionally reported as "the" bipartite GED upper bound.

## 1. Implementation sanity check (exact GED)

300 random triples, `n ∈ {3,4,5}`, `p=0.5`: exact GED is symmetric on
300/300, obeys the triangle inequality on 300/300, and `GED(A,A)=0` on
300/300. The exact-GED implementation is validated as a metric before it is
used as ground truth below.

## 2. Upper-bound property (300 pairs, `n ≤ 5`)

| quantity | count | % |
|---|---:|---:|
| `d_BP ≥ GED` | 300 / 300 | 100.0% |
| `d_BP = GED` (exact) | 170 / 300 | 56.7% |

`d_BP` never undershoots the exact value on this sample, as expected for an
edit path that is actually realisable.

## 3. Symmetry (2000 pairs, `n ∈ {3,4,5,6}`)

| quantity | asymmetric pairs | % |
|---|---:|---:|
| raw assignment value | 0 / 2000 | 0.0% |
| induced `d_BP` | 767 / 2000 | 38.35% |

The raw LSAP value is perfectly symmetric here (swapping `A,B` transposes
the block-structured cost matrix; the optimal assignment *value* is
transpose-invariant). The **induced** `d_BP` is not: the Hungarian solver
breaks ties differently by direction, so the correspondence read off
`LSAP(A,B)` need not be the inverse of the one from `LSAP(B,A)`, giving
different real edit-path costs. **This is the practically reported
quantity, and it is asymmetric on 38.35% of pairs.**

## 4. Triangle inequality (5000 triples, `n ∈ {3,4,5,6}`)

| quantity | violations | % |
|---|---:|---:|
| raw assignment value | 0 / 5000 | 0.0% |
| induced `d_BP` | 68 / 5000 | 1.36% |

Raw obeys the triangle inequality on every sampled triple here (consistent
with §3: an optimal-assignment cost over per-node terms that are each
themselves a metric on integers/labels tends to inherit the triangle
inequality). **Induced `d_BP` violates it on 1.36% of sampled triples.**

## 5. Contrast: exact GED never violates the triangle inequality

Of the 5000 triples above, 2124 had all three graphs with `n ≤ 5`
(exact-GED-feasible). Re-checked with exact GED on exactly these 2124
triples: **0 / 2124 violations (0.0%)**, confirming §1 and the theoretical
guarantee that exact GED is a metric.

## 6. Smallest counterexample found (induced `d_BP`, fewest total vertices = 10)

```
A: n=3, labels=[a,b,a], edges=[(0,2)]
B: n=3, labels=[b,b,b], edges=[(0,1)]
C: n=4, labels=[b,b,b,a], edges=[(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,2)]

d_BP(A,B) = 2
d_BP(B,C) = 4
d_BP(A,C) = 7   >   d_BP(A,B) + d_BP(B,C) = 6
```

`A` and `B` are both 3-vertex graphs with one edge (nearly isomorphic up to
labels); `C` is a 4-vertex graph with a triangle plus a pendant. The direct
`A→C` assignment happens to induce a strictly costlier edit path than
routing through `B`, which is exactly what a valid metric forbids.

## Conclusion

The Riesen–Bunke **induced** bipartite/Hungarian GED, `d_BP` — the quantity
conventionally reported as the approximate graph edit distance — is **not a
metric** on this cost model: asymmetric on 38.35% of 2000 random pairs and
violating the triangle inequality on 1.36% of 5000 random triples (strict,
integer costs, no tolerance ambiguity), while still satisfying `d_BP ≥ GED`
and `GED(A,A)=0`. The smallest violating triple found has 10 vertices total
(3+3+4), given above with all three pairwise distances as a ready-made
counterexample. The **raw** LSAP value, in contrast, was symmetric and
triangle-compliant on every sample tested — a pseudometric, but not an
achievable edit-path cost, and not what the literature reports as "the"
bipartite GED. Exact GED, independently verified as a metric on 300
sanity triples, never violates the triangle inequality on the 2124
exact-feasible triples drawn from the same population, so the failure is
specific to the bipartite relaxation's derived edit path.

## §3 Ambient reach and ball-coverage consensus (gate G-L3)

### Report: ambient reach and ball-coverage consensus (gate G-L3, run 2026-09-03)

Same 24 profiles, seeds and generators as the consensus pilot (§1): 12 planted
centres `M_0` (6 at `(n,m) = (8,10)`, 6 at `(10,12)`, 3-uniform, trivial
labels, `k = 3`), noise `t ∈ {1, 2}` connectivity-preserving Qin edits per
copy plus a random relabelling, `N = 7` copies. Scripts `reach_probe.py`,
`reach_analyze.py`; raw results `reach_results.json`; log `reach_log.txt`.
C++ engine; 252,685 canonicalizations in 643 s of CPU (max 0.174 s, **0 DNF**);
wall clock 3,023 s. Enumeration of `B_r(w)`: token-level Levenshtein ball over
the 13-symbol `k = 3` alphabet, words deduplicated, every word decoded by S2H,
decoded hypergraphs deduplicated by canonical key.

**Definitions.** `reach(K → K') = min{ r : class(K') ∈ classes(S2H(B_r(w*_c(K)))) }`,
computed for `r ∈ {1, 2}` (else "> 2"). `cov_r(M) = #{ i : reach(M → K_i) ≤ r }`
over the 7 copies. A copy candidate covers itself for free (`w ∈ B_1(w)`), so
coverage is reported raw and with self-coverage excluded. Candidate set for

#### Reading

1. **Reach is small exactly where `d_I` is large — for one-edit variants.**
   A copy that differs from the centre by one structural edit (plus a
   relabelling) sits, in `d_I`, 11–19 tokens away (≈ 50 % of the word); yet
   40 % (8,10) and 26 % (10,12) of such copies are **one token edit** away
   from `w*_c(M_0)` through a non-canonical word, and 52–55 % are within two.
   The vocabulary's `d_amb` argument is confirmed: the avalanche is a
   property of the *canonical-to-canonical* comparison, not of the ambient
   space. For two-edit variants the enumerable radius (`r ≤ 2`) no longer
   suffices (83–86 % beyond reach 2): a structural edit costs one
   construction token plus pointer displacement, so two edits typically cost
   three to five tokens.
2. **Reach is asymmetric in the centre's favour.** Centre → copy at `r = 1`
   is 26–40 %; copy → copy is 2–3 %; copy → centre is 21–24 %. The centre is
   the point *from which* the others are cheapest to write — a generative
   notion of consensus that the metric medoid does not capture.
3. **Ball coverage singles out the planted centre for one-edit variants,
   where the `d_I`-medoid never does.** `M_0` is a coverage maximiser at
   `r = 1` in 11/12 one-edit profiles and the unique maximiser in 9/12; the
   medoid covers 0–0.33 copies once its free self-coverage is removed. At
   `r = 2` the gap widens (3.8 vs 2.0 and 3.7 vs 1.5 copies) and the
   truncated reach sum is lower for `M_0` in both cells. For two-edit
   variants the layer fails (coverage 0.17 at `r = 1`, ties with the copies).
4. **Cost.** `|B_1| ≈ 570–740` words (280–360 distinct classes) in under a
   second per origin; `|B_2| ≈ 1.6–2.7 × 10⁵` words (30–55 K classes) in
   14–54 s per origin at these sizes; `r = 3` is not enumerable without
   pruning. The reach layer is therefore a **single-fact-resolution
   instrument**: exact neighbourhood queries and consensus recovery for
   collections whose members differ from a common source by about one
   instruction — and it is computable only because every enumerated word
   decodes (0 failures in 425,000 decoded words across both pilots).

**Verdict for the storyline.** Branch A holds, scoped to single-edit
resolution: the ambient layer resolves what `d_I` cannot, at radius ≤ 2, and
only a decodable representation can compute it. Two-edit and larger
disagreements are outside the enumerable radius at the current alphabet;
that is the stated limitation and the motivation for the semantics-aligned
alphabet as future work.

## 5. What this means for the consensus experiment

**Dataset: `contact-high-school`. Drop `contact-primary-school`** — no ego-net
below `n = 21`, sparsest has `m = 253`, all 9 sampled instances timed out in
both modes; it cannot supply a single KB.

**Size cap: cap `m`, not `n`.** The safe selector is `m ≤ 110` (20/21 complete,
median 0.03 s); an `n`-cap does not control cost — `n ≤ 24` still admits
`m = 307` instances that are dead. **Groups surviving with `N ≥ 5`** (HS):

| corpus rule | KBs | groups `N ≥ 5` | groups `N ≥ 3` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `n ≤ 16` | 19 | 1 (MP\*1 5) | 3 |
| `n ≤ 24` | 59 | 6 | 8 |
| **`m ≤ 110` (safe)** | **26** | **1** — MP\*1 (12) | 3 — MP\*1 12, PSI\* 4, PC 3 |
| `m ≤ 150` (mixed) | 41 | 2 — MP\*1 18, PSI\* 6 | 6 |
| `m ≤ 200` (expect DNFs) | 64 | 4 — MP\*1 21, PSI\* 15, 2BIO1 7, 2BIO2 6 | 8 |
| `m ≤ 250` (many DNFs) | 76 | 7 | 8 |

In the regime where `w*_c` reliably completes (`m ≤ 110`), **exactly one class
group reaches `N ≥ 5`** — MP\*1, 12 KBs. That is one consensus instance, not a
flagship. Pushing to `m ≤ 200` buys 4 groups, but ~20 % of those will time out,
and the DNFs are not random — they are the near-symmetric instances, so the
selection is structurally biased.

**Labelled faster than unlabelled? Yes** — median 1.91×, faster in 28/32 paired
completions, aggregate 1.67×, plus two DNF→sub-3 s rescues. Use the labelled
form; strictly better, but it buys a factor, not a frontier.

**Two design consequences.** (1) All 64 ego-nets with `n ≤ 24` are pairwise
non-isomorphic in both modes — a median KB has no repeated exemplar to recover,
so any recovered median is a novel structure that cannot be scored against a
planted ground truth; the experiment needs a ground-truth story that does not
assume repeats. (2) Connectivity is free (0/569 exceptions).

**Bottom line.** The gates do not pass as hoped: `contact-high-school` yields a
single label group with ≥ 5 canonicalisable KBs at the safe density cap, and the
real ego-nets are far denser (`m` in the hundreds at `n ≈ 20–30`) than the
`w*_c` engine handles. Options: (a) run the flagship on planted corpora with
MP\*1 as a small real anchor; (b) sparsify the ego-nets (keep only maximal
hyperedges, or threshold by contact frequency) and re-measure — untested here;
(c) find a labeled ARB dataset with lower incidence density. This mirrors the
OQ-B / T-DQ3' HIC outcome in `docs/article/DATA.md` §2 — the real anchor is a
small-corpus anchor, not a scale claim.

## 6. What this means

**Does WD50K give thousands of ego-KBs inside the envelope?** Yes, structurally. Reading (a),
subject-only: **9,461 ego-KBs with n ≤ 24** (7,301 at n ≤ 16, 5,778 at n ≤ 12), or 21,480 at n ≤ 24
under the incident reading. Reading (b), the E2 anchored incidence encoding, is much more expensive:
median n' = 27, and only 5,884 / 12,747 (46 %) fit n' ≤ 24 — E2 roughly doubles the vertex count and
is arity-2 by construction, so it inherits the star-symmetry blow-up without the label ties to break
it. E2 was **not** timed here (the task scoped timing to reading (a)); on this evidence it is the
worse of the two readings for canonicalization cost.

**Does the hypergraph machinery matter here?** Only marginally. **13.6 % of statements have any
qualifier**, and among the in-envelope ego-KBs **72.7 % are pure graphs** (max arity 2) — a WD50K
subject ego-KB is, typically, a labelled star *graph*. 2,587 in-envelope ego-KBs (27.3 %) do carry at
least one arity-≥3 edge, which is enough to populate a hypergraph corpus, but a reviewer can fairly
say the bulk of this dataset is a graph problem. Also, 62 statements (and 1 sampled ego-KB) exceed
`K_MAX = 10` and are simply not encodable; any WD50K-derived corpus must declare an arity cap.

**Labelled vs unlabelled cost.** The asymmetry is the headline. With relation labels the envelope is
real: median 0.0003–0.17 s and a 2.5 % DNF rate up to n = 24, worst observed 48 s. Without labels the
envelope collapses at n ≈ 13: 33 % DNF at 60 s and 3-consecutive-DNF aborts in every bucket above 12.
Anonymous star-shaped real KB fragments are close to the adversarial case for tie-complete branching
(the avalanche/drift regime the article already documents). Any WD50K experiment must therefore run
**labelled**, and must still expect a small DNF tail on label-degenerate stars (one relation repeated
across many spokes); a per-instance timeout with censoring is mandatory.

**Structural repetition.** Real KB collections repeat structure heavily, but almost all of that
repetition is *shape*, not *content*: unlabelled, 9,461 ego-KBs collapse to 764 classes with the top
10 covering 54 % (stars of a given size); labelled, they spread to 8,044 classes with 7,608
singletons and the largest class at 138. So a labelled corpus gives near-unique fingerprints — good
for a retrieval/kNN framing, poor for consensus/outlier framings that need populated classes; an
unlabelled corpus gives dense repetition but is computationally out of reach above n ≈ 12 and is
dominated by trivial star classes. A middle option (coarsened relation vocabulary, e.g. relation
type-groups) is the obvious knob and was not measured.

**Bottom line.** WD50K supplies thousands of small, connected, labelled ego-KBs that canonicalize in
milliseconds — but they are mostly labelled stars with 13.6 % hyper-relational content. It is a
viable *real-data anchor* for a labelled retrieval-style application, not a convincing showcase of
high-arity hypergraph structure, and it is unusable in the unlabelled regime beyond n ≈ 12.

### What this means

**Largest in-envelope set of genuinely n-ary KBs.** Two answers. By raw count of in-envelope KBs holding at least one arity-≥3 edge, **wd50k_33 wins with 3,932** (wd50k_66 3,167, WD50K 2,584, JF17K 2,499, WikiPeople 2,354, wd50k_100 1,847) — it is simply the largest corpus of the family. But its hyperedge *content* is thin: 17.9 % of in-envelope edges have arity ≥ 3, so a typical member is a labelled star graph with one or two n-ary spokes. **wd50k_66 is the better operating point** — 3,167 n-ary KBs at 54.7 % arity-≥3 edges and 79.3 % of KBs hyper-relational, with a 13 % DNF rate no worse than wd50k_33's. **wd50k_100 is the purity extreme**: 1,847 KBs, 99.8 % of edges arity ≥ 3, 100 % of KBs hyper-relational, median `n = 11`, sub-second below `n ≈ 17` — the only collection where a reviewer cannot say "this is a graph problem". Against §5's WD50K (27.3 % of in-envelope KBs hyper-relational), **wd50k_100 raises hyper-relational purity from 27 % to 100 % at 1/5 the corpus size, and wd50k_66 to 79 % at 1.2×**. JF17K and WikiPeople are worse on every axis that matters: JF17K has the highest DNF rate (33 %) and its qualifier roles carry no information beyond the main relation; WikiPeople is 88 % plain triples, its stars are the densest (median `m = 16`), and only 24 % of its in-envelope KBs are hyper-relational.

**Do entity types give usable class labels?** Yes, with a balance caveat. P31 coverage is essentially total (100.0 % on wd50k_100, 99.8 % pooled) and cheap. The pooled 10,000-anchor set has **98 types with ≥ 20 members covering 8,616 entities (86.2 %)** — ample for a kNN/classification exhibit. Restricted to wd50k_100 alone the label set collapses to **8 usable classes covering 1,666 entities (90.2 %)**, severely imbalanced: `human` (925) and `film` (572) are 81 % of the labelled mass and four of the remaining six are near-synonyms of "country". So wd50k_100 supports at best a 2–4 class problem unless labels are coarsened; the pooled WD50K population is where a genuine multi-class label set exists. P31 is also multi-valued, which needs a policy (most frequent, or first in a fixed ontology order).

**Structural repetition.** Under the labelled reading, very little in the WD family and none to speak of in WikiPeople: labelled classes per in-envelope KB are 0.92 (wd50k_33), 0.92 (wd50k_66), 0.89 (WikiPeople), 0.85 (WD50K), **0.80 (wd50k_100)**, with the largest class holding ≤ 8.3 % of the corpus. On the n-ary subsets it is worse for the big corpora (0.98 on wd50k_33 and WikiPeople, 0.97 on WD50K) and unchanged for wd50k_100 (0.80). **JF17K is the outlier and the only collection with real repetition**: 0.358 classes/KB overall, 0.425 on the n-ary subset, top-10 classes covering 18.6 % (28.9 % n-ary) — its role-typed vocabulary puts many entities into structurally identical KBs. Stripping labels produces heavy but trivial repetition everywhere (595–1,397 classes, top-10 covering 24–54 %) — stars of a given size — and §5 already measured that regime as out of reach above `n ≈ 12`. Practical consequence, unchanged from §5: **labelled corpora give near-unique fingerprints, good for retrieval/kNN framings and poor for consensus/outlier framings that need populated classes.** JF17K is the one exception, and its repetition is bought at a 33 % DNF rate. Qualifier folding is the wrong knob in the other direction: it refines an already near-singleton census by 0–3 % while tripling the alphabet.

### Proof sketches (agent draft, checked)

#### Proof sketches for `Σ⁺` — Propositions 2⁺ and 4

Notation. VM state `S = (H, L, p_1 … p_k)`; `n(S) = |V(H)|`. Vertices are
numbered in creation order, `rank(v) = ` creation index, seed = rank 0
(`SparseHypergraph.add_node` appends, so `rank ≡ NodeId`). Clamp
`ρ_S(r) = min(max(r,0), n(S)−1)`. `Σ⁺ = Σ_HG(k) ∪ {A, A⁺}`.

##### Proposition 2⁺

**(i) Conservativity.** `S2H⁺ ↾ Σ_HG* = S2H`.
*Proof.* The `S2H⁺` step function on `W, P, N, C, V` is defined by the same
operations on the same components as `S2H` (same `CircularDoublyLinkedList`,
same `KPointerSet`, same `add_node` / `add_hyperedge` call sequence, same
CDLL pre-allocation `1 + Σ_V j`). Induction on the token count gives identical
states after every prefix, hence identical outputs. ∎
*Measured:* 2 000 random `Σ_HG(3)` words — 2 000/2 000 identical structural keys;
1 891 canonicalizable, 1 891/1 891 identical `w*_c`.

**(ii) Totality.** Every `w ∈ Σ⁺*` executes without raising.
*Proof.* Induct on prefixes with invariant `I(S)`: `n(S) ≥ 1`, the CDLL holds
exactly the `n(S)` vertices, every `p_i` is a live slot. The initial state (seed
alone) satisfies `I`. Package tokens preserve `I` (Prop. 2 of the preprint).
`A[ℓ; r_1…r_a]`: `ρ_S(r) ∈ [0, n−1]` is a live vertex because `n ≥ 1`; the
support `ρ_S({r_i})` is non-empty because `a ≥ 1`, so `add_hyperedge` is legal;
a repeated `(ℓ, support)` is absorbed by the edge lookup; no CDLL or pointer
change, so `I` holds. `A⁺[ℓ; λ; r_1…r_i]`: the clamp is evaluated at `n ≥ 1`
(**hypothesis A1**, see below), then one vertex is appended and inserted after
the live slot `p_1`, so `I` holds. Capacity is never exceeded: the CDLL is
pre-allocated to `1 + Σ_V j + #A⁺`, exactly the number of vertices `w` can
create. ∎

**(iii) Connectivity.** `S2H⁺(w)` is connected for every `w ∈ Σ⁺*`.
*Proof.* Induction with "every vertex lies in the seed's primal component".
Base: the one-vertex hypergraph. `W, P, N` leave `H` alone. `C` and `A` add an
edge over pre-existing vertices — adding an edge never disconnects. `V` adds
`j` fresh vertices inside one edge that also contains the pre-existing `p_1`
(`i ≥ 1`), so they join `p_1`'s component. `A⁺` adds one fresh vertex inside one
edge that also contains `ρ_S(r_1)`, a pre-existing vertex (`i ≥ 1`), so it joins
that component. ∎
**Hypothesis the design does not state:** `a ≥ 1` for `A` *and* `i ≥ 1` for
`A⁺` are load-bearing. With `i = 0`, `A⁺` would create an isolated vertex and
(iii) would fail. The stated ranges `1 ≤ a ≤ k`, `1 ≤ i ≤ k−1` already give this.
*Measured:* 20 000 random `Σ⁺(3)` words of length 1–40 with arbitrary
(out-of-range 97.3 %, repeated, unsorted) ranks — 0 exceptions, 0 disconnected,
0 duplicate edges.

##### Proposition 4 (fact-level simulation, anchored encoding `E1⊤`)

Setting. `K` a knowledge base, `E1⊤(K) = H`: anchor `⊤`, a `dom` edge `{⊤, c}`
per constant, facts as hyperedges labelled `1…r`. `w = w*_c(H)`,
`H_0 = S2H(w) ≅ H` the rank-numbered copy, `a = rank_{H_0}(⊤)`.

**(a) Insertion costs one token.** For a fact `f = (ℓ, S)`, `S ⊆ constants(H_0)`,
`1 ≤ |S| ≤ k`, `f ∉ E(H_0)`: `S2H⁺(w · A[ℓ; S]) = H_0 + f`, and
`d_Lev(w, w·A[ℓ;S]) = 1`.
*Proof.* At the end of `w` the vertex count is `n(H_0)`, and every `s ∈ S` is
the rank of an existing vertex, so no clamp fires (**hypothesis H1**); `A` has
no other effect. ∎ *Measured:* 1 200/1 200 (60 KBs × 20 sampled insertions).

*Position independence (used by the ball argument and by the Task-4 reduction).*
`A` touches neither the CDLL nor the pointers, and edge insertion commutes with
every later operation, so inserting `A[ℓ;S]` at **any** position `p` yields
`S2H⁺(w) + (ℓ, ρ_{S_p}(S))`. Hence the set of objects reachable by inserting one
`A` token equals `{ S2H⁺(w) + (ℓ,S') : |S'| ≤ k }`.

**(b) Deletion of a `C`-created fact costs one token.** If the first token of
`w` that creates `f` is `C` at position `p`, and no other token of `w` creates
`f` (**hypothesis H2**, uniqueness of the creator), then
`S2H⁺(w \ p) = H_0 − f`.
*Proof.* `C` changes neither the CDLL nor the pointers, so removing it leaves
every other token's execution bit-identical; the only difference is the absent
edge. ∎ *Measured:* 710/710 (`t3`) + 463/463 (`t3b`); 0 creator-misses in 1 197
facts, so H2 held everywhere on `w*_c`.

**(c) Deletion of a `V`-created fact costs `j` tokens.** If `f` is first created
by `V[ℓ; i; j; λ_1…λ_j]` at position `p`, then
`w' = w[:p] · A⁺[dom; λ_1; a] ⋯ A⁺[dom; λ_j; a] · w[p+1:]`
satisfies `S2H⁺(w') ≅ H_0 − f`, at token cost `j` (one substitution plus `j−1`
insertions).
*Proof.* Both programs create exactly `j` vertices at position `p` and insert
each of them after the same slot `p_1`, so after position `p` the two CDLLs have
the same length and the same cyclic order *of positions*; let `φ` be the induced
position bijection. Under `φ` the two partial states differ only in that the `V`
run holds the edge `f` while the `A⁺` run holds the `j` edges `{⊤, u_t}`. Under
`E1⊤` each of those `dom` edges is created again later in `w`, so the `A⁺` run's
final edge set is `E(H_0) − {f}`; every later token acts through pointer
positions, which `φ` preserves. ∎
**Hypotheses:** H3 — `⊤` already exists at position `p` (`a < n` there), so the
`A⁺` clamp does not fire; H4 — the `j` fresh vertices are constants, not `⊤`.
**Correction the design needs (measured, not hypothetical).** `A⁺` inserts each
fresh vertex *after `p_1`*, so a run of `j` such tokens lays the block out in the
**reverse** CDLL order from `V`: for `j = 2`, `V` gives CDLL `[0,1,2]` and the
`A⁺` run gives `[0,2,1]` (`t3b` part 1). The witness is nevertheless correct,
because at the instant of creation the `j` fresh vertices are mutually
interchangeable (each carries exactly one new `dom` edge), so `φ` is an
automorphism of the partial state and only *renames* the block. What is **not**
preserved is the block's rank numbering: `φ` reverses it. Consequences: the
witness realizes `H_0 − f` **up to a rank permutation of the `j` fresh
constants**, so an iterated (multi-step) witness must recompute ranks after each
`A⁺` run; and if the design ever wants `S2H⁺(w') = H_0 − f` on the nose rather
than up to isomorphism, `A⁺` must insert after the previously inserted fresh
vertex when it directly follows another `A⁺` (or the block must be emitted in
reverse). *Measured:* 5/5 (`t3`) + 17/17 (`t3b`) `j = 2` witnesses correct
up to isomorphism; 0 failures.

**(d) Ball inclusions.** Write `B_r(w)` for the token-Levenshtein ball and
`reach(K → K') ≤ r` for `class(K') ∈ classes(S2H⁺(B_r(w*_c(K))))`.
1. If `K'` is `K` plus `r` fact insertions, then `reach(K → K') ≤ r`: append the
   `r` `A` tokens of (a); the appends commute and no clamp fires, so the ball of
   radius `r` contains a witness.
2. If `K'` is `K` after `r` fact edits, then `reach(K → K') ≤ (k−1)r`: each
   insertion costs 1 by (a) and each deletion costs `1` or `j ≤ k−1` by (b)/(c).
Both directions are **inclusions only**. The converse fails: a pointer-token
edit rewrites the decoded object globally, so `reach ≤ r` does not bound the
number of fact edits.
*Measured (`t4`, 48 profiles):* family I, `t = 1` — `cov_1(H_0) = 7/7` in 12/12
profiles; family I, `t = 2` — `cov_1(H_0) = 0/7`, `cov_2(H_0) = 7/7` in 12/12.

##### Hypotheses the design did not state

1. **A1** — `A⁺` clamps against the vertex count *before* creating the fresh
   vertex (otherwise an out-of-range rank collapses onto the fresh vertex and the
   edge loses arity).
2. `a ≥ 1` / `i ≥ 1` are needed for connectivity (Prop. 2⁺ iii).
3. **H1** (no clamping on insertion witnesses), **H2** (unique creator token),
   **H3**/**H4** (the anchor precedes the replaced `V` token and is not among
   its fresh vertices).
4. The design says "attach each fresh constant to the anchor **at rank 0**".
   The anchor is the canonical seed only when it wins the neighbour-degree
   cascade outright — measured 295/300 (98.3 %) on `E1⊤` KBs with 8–12
   constants; the other 1.7 % put the anchor at rank 1. Witnesses must address
   the anchor by its **measured** rank, not by the constant 0.
5. The rank-reversal caveat of (c).
6. Clause (c) is a theorem **about `E1⊤`**, not about `Σ⁺`. On a non-anchored
   encoding there are no `dom` edges to re-attach the `j` fresh constants to, so
   a `V`-created fact has *no* bounded-cost deletion witness at all — measured
   in `t4`, where family-D coverage is capped by the `C`-created share of the
   edges (mean 4.5–8.2 of 8–14 edges).

### Coordinator's reading

**The criterion is met, by E-B, in a characterized regime — and my own
hypothesis was wrong.** I predicted local addressing (E-C) would be the stable
one because a refinement is local. It is the worst of the three, and the
mechanism is measured rather than guessed: a depth-3 hypergraph-WL colour is a

**Under the pre-registered rule (best measured local stability wins), E-B is
selected.** A fact insertion over existing constants costs **exactly one
token** on all three corpora, against 5–15 tokens and half the word under the
pointer alphabet; canonicalization drops by two to five orders of magnitude
and stops censoring.

**The boundary, which the paper must state as a result rather than bury.**
E-B's locality holds for edits that leave the constant set alone and degrades
to the pointer alphabet's level when an edit adds or strands a constant, because
*any* isomorphism-invariant total order renumbers when the set it orders
changes. On the WD50K constant-preserving ladders E-B is decisive (Spearman
0.633 vs 0.563; Δ=1 median 1 token vs 5; 59.5 % within 2 tokens vs 7.5 %). On
the **natural** NDC quarterly series, where consecutive quarters routinely gain
and lose classes, E-B is *not* better than E-A (Spearman 0.632 vs 0.683; Δ=1
median 7 vs 4). A follow-up splits that series by whether the constant set
changed, to size the regime the consensus experiment actually runs in.

**Consequences accepted.** E-B correlates with the nauty-certificate edit
distance at ρ = 0.62–0.96 — it *is* a canonical labelling serialized to a
sorted fact list — so, exactly as pre-registered, the article's distinctiveness
now rests on the language properties (total decoding, ball enumeration, the
certified ball-coverage representative, generation with validity 1) and that
argument must be written explicitly. Compactness is mixed: F4 is shorter on
dense knowledge bases (25 vs 38 tokens) and about twice as long on the sparse
real ones, because it pays one type token per constant where the pointer
encoding amortizes constants into the tokens that create them.

**Roles addendum.** The role-carrying variant of E-C is identical to E-C to the
last digit — E-C is saturated at zero shared symbols, so roles cost and buy
nothing *there*. The role cost under the adopted E-B is **not yet measured**
and is a follow-up.

### Report: F4 topology probe — does a fact-addressed encoding make similar knowledge bases close?

2026-09-04. Decisive probe for the alphabet change (`logic_models/encoding.md` §3.1, absolute
vs relative addressing), against one criterion: **knowledge bases one fact apart must be at
small Levenshtein distance**. Everything lives in this directory; no repository file outside it
was touched.

#### 1. Design as implemented

A knowledge base `K` is a labelled hypergraph — constants are vertices with a type label, facts
are hyperedges with a predicate label and are sets of constants (`f4_encodings.KB`). All
distances are unweighted Levenshtein over atomic symbols (rapidfuzz): one token = one symbol,
equality is equality of the whole `(kind, label, tuple)` triple.

**E-A — status quo.** `canonical_string(H, k, algorithm="canonical", backend="cpp")`; distance
over `instructions.parse(w*_c)` with the `("seed", label)` prefix when the vertex vocabulary is
non-trivial, mirroring `metric_space/distances/isalhg_levenshtein.py`; `k` = pair max of
`required_k`. **E-B — F4, global canonical ranks.** `pynauty.canon_label` on the coloured Levi
graph (`core/levi_reduction.to_levi`: vertex colour = type, edge colour = `|Σ_v| + predicate`),
restricted to the constant-side nodes in canonical order; `rank[v]` is `v`'s position there.
Word = type prefix `T[type(π⁻¹(r))]`, `r = 0 … n−1`, then one `F[ℓ; r_1 < … < r_a]` per fact,
fact tokens sorted by `(ℓ, rank tuple)`. `canon_label` returns a permutation, so there were no
residual ties to break. **E-C — F4, local addresses.** `addr[v] = (wl_hash(H,
max_rounds=3)[v], index)`, the index ordering same-coloured constants by their E-B rank. Word =
type prefix in increasing address order, then `F[ℓ; (col,idx)_1 … (col,idx)_a]`, operands sorted
by address, fact tokens sorted lexicographically.

**Choices the specification left open — all of them.** (1) *E-C type prefix* = `T[(col,idx); type]`, address inside the token: in E-B the
key is the rank and position carries it, and an address-keyed prefix is what makes E-C demonstrably complete (a degree-0 constant is
otherwise unrecoverable). (2) *WL depth `h = 3`* = `max_rounds=3` (`core/hypergraph_wl`, C++); colours are raw FNV-1a values, not ranks
among those present, so an address means the same thing in two different KBs. (3) *Normalizer* = `d / |w(base)|`. (4) *Deletions*: a
knowledge base *is* its set of facts, so `delete_fact` / `remove_constant` drop now-isolated constants and renumber
(`f4_corpora.compact`); edits with a disconnected result are rejected and resampled, since `canonical_string` rejects disconnected input
and all arms must see identical instances. (5) *Edit budget*: "up to 10 edits per KB" read as up to 10 **per edit kind**, ≤ 50 per base
KB. (6) *Labels*: NDC types = 4 values (`[epc]`, `[moa]`, `[pe]`, untyped), ids from sorting those names, one predicate; WD50K(66)
predicate = `s.relation` (unfolded, as `probe_hyperrel.star_spec`), constants untyped. (7) *WD50K ladders (M2)*: no natural series
exists, so ladders are synthetic **constant-set-preserving** fact insert/delete walks, `t = 1…5` — fixing the constant ids makes
`Δ = |F_0 △ F_t|` exact, as the named ARB node ids do for NDC. (8) *E-A subsampling*: M1 scores E-A on the first 300 / 60 / 200 base KBs,
E-B and E-C on every pair; censoring is 60 s per instance via a killed forked child (`f4_exec.py`). The full-scale arm went to Picasso.

**Corpora.** synthetic — 2,000 (M0) / 300 (M1), `n ∈ [6,16]`, `m ∈ [6,20]`, arity 2–4, 3 types, 3 predicates, rejection sampling.

#### 9. Verdict

**(1) Does either fact-addressed encoding make one-fact-apart KBs close, against E-A's ~50 %?** **E-B yes, substantially; E-C no,
catastrophically.** E-A's pooled single-edit response is exactly 50 % of the word on all three corpora — the drift/avalanche figure,
reproduced. E-B cuts it to a normalized median of 0.188 (synthetic), 0.211 (NDC), 0.300 (WD50K), and for the purest one-fact edits it is
genuinely **one token**: median 1 for `insert_fact` on all three corpora, 1–2 for `delete_fact` and `add_constant` on synthetic. On the
WD50K ladders, constant set held fixed, 59.5 % of one-fact-apart pairs sit within 2 tokens under E-B against 7.5 % under E-A. E-C moves
the whole word: normalized median 1.000 everywhere, 0 of 384 Δ=1 pairs within 5 tokens. **The gain is real but not uniform**: E-B wins on
edits that leave the constant set alone, and when an edit adds or strands a constant the canonical order renumbers and E-B falls back to
E-A's level or below — `insert_fact_new_constant` (nrm 0.385–0.567), `delete_fact` on the real corpora (0.333–0.357). On the NDC
*natural* series, where consecutive quarters routinely change the constant set and words run ~13 tokens, E-B's Δ=1 normalized median
(0.462) is no better than E-A's (0.429). Ball enumeration at radius 1–2 would find one-fact repairs under E-B for constant-preserving
edits and still miss those that introduce a constant.

**(2) Does E-C's local addressing beat E-B's global ranks, and where?** **Nowhere** — worse than E-B on every corpus, every edit kind and
both M2 series, and worse than the status quo E-A on the single-edit criterion. The mechanism is measured, not conjectured (§6): depth-3
hypergraph-WL colours are global functions of a connected KB this size, while the nauty canonical rank order is untouched by 68–85 % of
edits. Lowering the depth does not save it — even `h = 1` moves 36–52 % of colours. The hypothesis behind E-C is falsified.

**(3) What breaks or degrades?** *Completeness*: nothing — both F4 encodings are complete isomorphism invariants in measurement (0 false
merges, 0 false splits over 3,000 instances with real collisions) and isomorphism-invariant (0 violations). *Compactness*: mixed — F4 is
shorter than `w*_c` on dense KBs (25 vs 38 tokens at `m` med 15), about twice as long on the sparse real corpora (13–15 vs 7 at `m` med
3–5). *Cost*: strictly better — one nauty call (20–60 µs) against `w*_c`'s median 1.1–1.7 ms, p90 2.2–8.3 s and a 6 % censor rate at
60 s. *Differentiation from nauty*: the real casualty. E-B correlates with the nauty-certificate edit distance at ρ = 0.62–0.96 (≥ 0.75
on five of seven pair sets) — expected, because E-B *is* a canonical labelling serialized to a fact list. E-C is the arm that stays
distinct from nauty (ρ 0.28–0.45 on single-edit pairs), and it is distinct by being useless. Under the pre-registered rule (best measured
local stability wins even if its distance is statistically indistinguishable from the nauty-certificate distance), **the measurement
selects E-B** — and the differentiation argument must then rest on the language (decodability, ball enumeration, generation), not on the
distance differing from nauty's.

**Corrected reading.** On constant-preserving pairs the adopted encoding
delivers exactly what the synthetic ladders promised: a one-fact difference is
**one token**, 77 % of such pairs sit within two tokens, and the normalized
distance is **7× tighter** than the pointer alphabet's. On constant-changing
pairs it is **worse than the pointer alphabet** — 0 % within two tokens against
40 %, and its distance tracks *the number of constants moved* (ρ = 0.503,
above the pointer alphabet's 0.430) rather than the number of facts changed.
The earlier pooled NDC figure of 0.211 is therefore an average over a mix in
which the adopted encoding loses three pairs out of four, and must not be
quoted on its own.

**Consequence for the consensus experiment (RQ3).** Restricted to
constant-preserving quarters, the NDC corpus collapses: of 172 drug classes
with an encodable quarter, **15 have a preserving run of ≥ 3 quarters and 2 of
≥ 5**, longest run 5. That is 15 series of 3–5 members — too small to carry
RQ3 alone. Either RQ3 runs on constant-preserving *ladders over real members*
(injected fact edits on real knowledge bases, keeping the constant set) with
the natural series reported as the harder case, or the substrate changes.
Recorded as an open scoping decision, not silently resolved.

**Where this leaves the design.** Neither encoding dominates on real variant
data: the pointer alphabet is content-addressed and pays globally for every
edit; the rank alphabet is position-addressed and pays globally for every
*constant*. That is a trade-off with a mechanism, and a third point —
positional addressing *within a locally determined class* — is being measured
to establish whether the trade-off is fundamental or merely unexplored.


### §9 second follow-up — the locally-keyed third point, and the obstruction

*Report: `f4_topology/probe_f4_local_key.md`. Two further encodings, both
complete iso-invariants in measurement (0 violations, 0 false merges or splits
over 3,000 instances): **E-D**, addressed by `(local key, index)` with the key
= (constant type, sorted multiset of `(predicate, arity)` over its incident
facts) — strictly depth-0, reading only the constant's own incidences — and
**E-D1**, the coarse control with key = (type, degree).*

**Both fail, and the failure completes the picture.** E-D **never** lands
within two tokens of a one-edit neighbour — 0 of 31,279 pairs — where the rank
encoding is within *one* token on 38–52 % of constant-preserving pairs.
Normalized single-edit response on preserving edits: rank 0.071 / 0.111 /
0.207 against E-D 0.643 / 0.727 / 0.667 and E-D1 0.500 / 0.412 / 0.500. E-D's
single win is monotonicity on the constant-changing split (ρ = 0.602 against
the pointer alphabet's 0.511 and the rank encoding's 0.281), but its median
distance at Δ = 1 is 10 tokens against 7, and its distance tracks constants
moved at ρ = 0.599 — *worse* than the 0.503 it had to beat. Cost is 1.2–1.4×
the rank encoding for identical token counts.

**The mechanism, and the sentence worth keeping.** The local key behaves
exactly as designed — a fact edit changes the key of 0.21–0.53 of the
constants, precisely the members of the edited fact — and it does not help,
because in every constant-preserving row the fraction of edits leaving the
address map *entirely* intact is **0.000**: no edit among 21,528 left it
untouched, since a fact insertion *is* a change to the incidence data the key
reads. The rank encoding's profile is bimodal instead: nothing moves at all on
57 / 43 / 22 % of preserving edits. **Locality is won by the mass of
zero-cost edits, not by a low average damage** — which is why an addressing
whose symbols are cheap to perturb loses to one whose symbols are usually not
perturbed at all.

**The obstruction, stated so a proposition can be built on it.** Let `A` map
constants to address symbols, isomorphism-invariantly.

- If `A` is **content-determined** (the symbol is a function of the constant's
  own incidence data), every fact edit changes `A` at the edit site, so the
  cost is `Θ(incidence mass touched)` and never zero — measured 0.000 zero-cost
  edits in 21,528.
- If `A` is **position-determined** (the symbol is a rank in an
  isomorphism-invariant total order), `A` is untouched with probability `p`
  under fact edits (measured `p` = 0.22–0.57), but any change to the constant
  set renumbers `Θ(|w|)`.
- **Hybrids pay both**: a position inside a content-determined class inherits
  the content cost at the edit site and the renumbering cost within the class.

An addressing that is `O(1)`-local under fact edits *and* under constant edits
would need an identifier that is not a function of the isomorphism class —
extrinsic naming, which is exactly what isomorphism-invariance forbids. This is
the frontier, and it is the article's finding rather than its embarrassment.

**Decision unchanged: the rank encoding stays adopted**, now for a measured
reason rather than a comparative one — it maximizes the fraction of edits that
cost nothing.
