Good — this is the parameter that most changes the character of the problem, so let me be precise about what actually changes and what doesn't. The short version: allowing the domain to **shrink** costs you almost nothing algorithmically, while allowing it to **grow** is the genuinely unbounded dimension you already have to loop over. But before that, letting the domain size vary forces you to fix a modeling choice you could previously leave implicit.

## The metric now forks

When the domain was fixed, "symmetric difference of ground atoms" was the whole story. Once the set of constants can change size, you have to decide whether a change to the *domain itself* is charged, or only changes to the *facts*:

- **(a) Fact-level / active-domain metric.** The domain is just the set of constants appearing in some fact. Distance = symmetric difference of atoms, full stop. Shrinking a constant means deleting all facts mentioning it, so it becomes invisible; a "bare" element with no facts simply doesn't exist. This is the clean Dalal lift you already had — nothing new to encode for shrink, because deletions already cover it.
- **(b) Element-level + fact-level metric.** Adding or removing a constant is charged separately from adding or removing facts, the way graph edit distance charges node insertions/deletions independently of edge edits. This is more expressive (you can say "dropping an entity is a big deal even if it had few facts") but requires an explicit presence predicate.

Which one you want is partly forced by your axioms. If Σ contains cardinality or pure-existence constraints untethered to any relation — "there exist at least two things," "at most k elements total" — then bare elements are semantically load-bearing and you essentially cannot use metric (a); raw domain size is observable and must be represented. If every axiom is tied to predicates, metric (a) is usually the right and simpler choice.

## Isomorphism invariance is still free — and this is the part worth checking

You might worry that a variable-size domain turns the distance into a maximum-common-subgraph / graph-edit-distance computation, since now you seemingly must *align* a KB and a candidate model of different sizes, and that alignment is itself NP-hard and GI-hard. It does not, and the reason is the same fixed-naming trick as before, extended to a domain of const(KB) plus a bounded pool of fresh anonymous elements.

Keep the KB's constant names pinned to fixed domain positions and let a candidate model B **reuse those names for the elements it keeps** and fresh names for elements it introduces. Because Σ is isomorphism-invariant, ranging over all labelings of B on this extended domain still covers every iso-class, so the labeled minimum equals the iso-invariant minimum. And there is never an incentive to give a kept element a name other than its KB name — relabeling is free, so a mismatched name only inflates the symmetric difference. An element the repair "keeps but overwrites" is identical to deleting its old facts and inserting new ones, which symmetric difference already counts. So the optimal alignment collapses to *identity on kept elements, fresh on new ones*, and you never solve general GED. The alignment that would make this hard is pinned by construction. Shrinking is realized as a KB constant going bare; growing as a fresh element becoming active.

## What actually changes in the encoding

Take the MaxSAT/ASP/ILP encoding from before and make three adjustments.

The search domain is const(KB) together with up to K fresh, fully interchangeable elements. Existential axioms are grounded over this extended domain; a fresh element becomes active exactly when the optimizer decides some ground atom about it is worth its cost. This is precisely the chase introducing labeled nulls to satisfy existential rules — your "grow" direction is chase-style value invention, and the repair literature on existential rules / TGDs with nulls is the closest match. If you're on metric (b), add a Boolean present(e) per element, a hard clause forcing present(e) whenever any atom mentions e, and a soft clause charging the toggling of present against its KB status; then pure-existence and cardinality axioms can constrain present(·) directly.

The domain bound survives essentially unchanged. If C is the cost of the best repair found so far, no repair needs more than C genuinely participating fresh elements, since each contributes at least one inserted fact hence at least one unit of cost. So you loop the fresh-element count from 0 up to at most C and never search a domain larger than |const(KB)| + C. Shrink imposes no analogous lower search bound — it's just deletions the optimizer already trades off — which is exactly why the shrink direction is "free" and the grow direction is the one requiring iterative deepening.

The symmetry situation inverts. Previously the only residual symmetry was Aut(KB), the interchangeable constants given the current facts. Now the dominant symmetry is the **full symmetric group on the fresh anonymous elements** — they are literally indistinguishable until the solver assigns facts to them, so without symmetry breaking you'll re-derive the same repair under every permutation of the fresh pool and waste enormous effort. This is the same interchangeable-domain-element symmetry that Paradox, Mace4, and Kodkod break natively, and on the CNF/ASP side BreakID or lex-leader constraints over the fresh block handle it. Breaking it well matters far more here than Aut(KB) breaking did in the fixed-domain case.

## Framing and literature

With both insertions and deletions permitted this is the **symmetric-difference / update-repair** setting (as opposed to subset- or superset-minimal repairs) — Wijsen's update repairs and the broader database-fixing line are the direct antecedent, and Katsuno–Mendelzon *update* (rather than revision) is the belief-change analogue once elements can appear and disappear. The grow-with-fresh-elements mechanism is the chase with nulls, so if your Σ is a set of existential rules, look at repair and consistency work over TGDs where nulls are first-class. The honest complexity statement is unchanged: still NP-hard at the optimization core and climbing into the second level of PH with quantifier alternation; variable domain size doesn't raise the class, it just adds the domain-size loop (bounded by C) and shifts where the symmetry lives.
