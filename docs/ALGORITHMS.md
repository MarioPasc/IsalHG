# ALGORITHMS.md — Algorithmic post-mortem of the preprint sweep

Author: M. Pascual-Gonzalez. Date: 2026-06-23.
Pair-read with `docs/PREPRINT.md` (sweep scope), `docs/DEVELOPMENT.md`
(open question #1), and `docs/CODE_DESIGN.md` (where the seven H2S
variants live).

## 1. Headline

The seven canonical-string algorithms shipped for the preprint sweep —
`greedy_single`, `greedy_min`, `greedy_min_inplace`, `greedy_min_wl_pruned`,
`greedy_min_inplace_wl_pruned`, `exhaustive`, `pruned_exhaustive` — are
**not competitive** with the Levi-reduction baselines (`pynauty_levi`,
`bliss_levi`, `traces_levi`) on any cell of the preprint cohort. The
preprint random-hypergraph sweep yields **DNF on 100% of (n, r, c) cells
across all 7 algorithms** (90 cells × 7 algorithms = 630 runs, all
TimeoutError at 600 s; results at
`/media/mpascual/Sandisk2TB/research/ISAL/isalhg/results/preprint/algorithms/full/analysis_full/cells.csv`).
The only items any IsalHG variant completed are the small symmetric
designs (Fano STS(7), STS(9), STS(13)/cyclic and GQ(2,2) doily) that
were carried in from the Tier-1 fixtures. Even there, the speed gap
against pynauty is **4 to 7 orders of magnitude** (§3).

The algorithmic premise — porting the IsalSR canonical-string
machinery from labeled DAGs to hypergraphs — is the proximate cause.
The fix is not parameter tuning; it is a different family of
algorithm (§5–6).

## 2. Empirical evidence

### 2.1 Preprint sweep — full wipeout on random hypergraphs

Cohort: `UniformErdosRenyiHypergraphs` with
`n ∈ {50, 200, 1000}`, `r ∈ {3, 5}`, `c ∈ {1, 5, 25}` (edges per vertex),
5 seeds per cell → 90 (n, r, c, seed) instances × 7 algorithms.
Per-instance budget: 600 s wall-clock (the preprint's per-fingerprint
timeout, matching `PROPOSAL.md` §6).

| Algorithm | n cells run | DNF | items completed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `greedy_single` | 90 | 90 (100%) | 0 random; 5 designs |
| `greedy_min` | 90 | 90 (100%) | 0 random; 2 designs |
| `greedy_min_inplace` | 90 | 90 (100%) | 0 random; 2 designs |
| `greedy_min_wl_pruned` | 90 | 90 (100%) | 0 random; 2 designs |
| `greedy_min_inplace_wl_pruned` | 90 | 90 (100%) | 0 random; 2 designs |
| `exhaustive` | 90 | 90 (100%) | 0 random; 2 designs |
| `pruned_exhaustive` | 90 | 90 (100%) | 0 random; 5 designs |

Even the smallest cell (`n=50, r=3, c=1`: ≈ 50 hyperedges over 50
vertices, sparser than any IsalSR DAG fixture) times out at 600 s on
every variant. The total wall-clock spent on DNFs alone is
≈ 105 hours (630 cells × 600 s).

### 2.2 Head-to-head against Levi baselines

Single-process, single-core, repeats=3, best-of-3 reported. Source
script: `scratchpad/head_to_head.py`. Same `pynauty 2.8.8.1`,
`python-igraph 1.0.0`, `dreadnaut` (nauty 2.9 via conda-forge) as the
sweep. ER instances generated by uniform-r Bernoulli sampling at
`p = c·n / C(n, r)`.

| Instance | n / m | IsalHG single-seed | IsalHG canonical | pynauty_levi | bliss_levi | traces_levi |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fano STS(7) | 7 / 7 | 91.1 ms | 641 ms | **24 μs** | 50 μs | 509 μs |
| STS(9) (AG(2,3)) | 9 / 12 | 684 ms | 6.13 s | **30 μs** | 71 μs | 484 μs |
| GQ(2,2) doily | 15 / 15 | 21.41 s | DNF (> 300 s) | **38 μs** | 83 μs | 492 μs |
| ER n=20, m=100 | 20 / 100 | DNF (> 120 s) | — | 589 μs | 277 ms | 818 μs |
| ER n=30, m=150 | 30 / 150 | DNF (> 120 s) | — | 289 μs | 558 μs | 1.02 ms |
| ER n=50, m=250 | 50 / 250 | DNF (> 120 s) | — | 531 μs | 945 μs | 1.14 ms |

Speed gaps relative to `pynauty_levi`:

- Fano: **3 800×** (single-seed) and **27 000×** (full canonical).
- STS(9): **23 000×** and **204 000×**.
- GQ(2,2): **> 560 000×** (single-seed); canonical did not finish.
- ER n=20: **>>200 000×** lower bound (IsalHG DNF, pynauty 600 μs).

The gap grows with both vertex count and edge density. On random
hypergraphs the absolute IsalHG wall-clock is unbounded above (DNF),
while pynauty grows roughly linearly in (n + m) and remains
sub-millisecond up to n = 200.

## 3. Root-cause analysis: why the IsalSR-inherited algorithms fail

The preprint algorithms were ported from
`/home/mpascual/research/code/IsalSR/src/isalsr/core/`. The
correspondence is one-to-one
(`canonical.py::canonical_string` ↔ IsalSR's `canonical_string`;
`canonical.py::pruned_canonical_string` ↔ IsalSR's; `algorithms/*`
named identically). The transfer carries four assumptions from the
DAG setting that do not hold for hypergraphs:

**3.1 Distinguished start vertex.** IsalSR's `canonical_string`
docstring states the simplification verbatim: *"since input variables
are distinguishable and `x_1` is a fixed, distinguished starting
node, we run D2S from `x_1` ONLY. No iteration over starting nodes"*
(`/home/mpascual/research/code/IsalSR/src/isalsr/core/canonical.py`,
L7–9). For a SR expression tree, `x_1` is a primary input — there is
no automorphism that maps `x_1` to anything else, so a single greedy
run is iso-equivariant by construction. Hypergraphs have no
distinguished vertex. The IsalHG `greedy_min` family compensates by
iterating greedy H2S from every `max_xi_nodes(H, depth=3)` seed and
taking the lex-min. On vertex-transitive designs (Fano, STS(9),
GQ(2,2)) every vertex has the same ξ, so the seed set is the *entire*
vertex set and the lex-min reduction multiplies wall-clock by n.

**3.2 Bounded branching factor inside a single trajectory.** In a
LabeledDAG, every internal node has out-degree ≤ 2 (binary operators)
and inputs are typed; `_step` in IsalSR's `canonical.py` (L427) sorts
candidates by `(label_char, WL_hash)` and greedily picks the
*unique* best when ties do not exist — backtracking fires only on
ties. In an unlabelled hypergraph every ξ-tied vertex with the same
WL hash is a tie, so backtracking does fire. The IsalHG H2S encoder
at `src/isalhg/core/hypergraph_to_string.py::_encode_from` (L262–427)
enumerates label-respecting permutations of the new-input vertices at
every `V` emission. The module docstring states the worst case
explicitly: *"the worst-case complexity is product of `j!` over
emissions"*. For a hypergraph with E edges of mean arity r emitted as
V tokens with j new inputs each, the upper bound is
`(r!)^{E·share_V}` — for random ER cells with E ≈ 5n this is
intractable already at n = 20 (see §2.2). For symmetric designs the
WL pruning that `greedy_min_*_wl_pruned` adds collapses to identity
because color refinement plateaus on vertex-transitive structures
(Böker 2019 [B19], `hypergraph_wl.py` docstring L19–24).

**3.3 Acyclic structure constrains forward progress.** IsalSR's
backtracking depth is bounded by the DAG's longest input-to-output
path (typically ≤ 20 for SR expressions). IsalHG hyperedges close
into cycles freely; the encoder must consider every displacement
within `radius = len(state.i2o)` (currently the full visited set).
`_enum_displacements(k, radius)` grows polynomially in radius but the
nested V-branch backtracking compounds.

**3.4 Implementation tier.** IsalSR's `_step` and `_fast_step` use
`@lru_cache`, raw-array unchecked accessors, and a 1-WL subtree hash
recomputed once (`canonical.py` L36–46 docstring; "uses unchecked
accessors (`node_label_unchecked`, `node_data_unchecked`,
`has_edge_unchecked`, `out_neighbors_raw`) to eliminate bounds-check
overhead in the backtracking hot path"). IsalHG's
`hypergraph_to_string.py` re-walks `state.cdll`, recomputes
`_label_respecting_perms`, and re-hashes WL signatures per branch.
Implementation overhead alone is roughly 10× — but that is the smallest
term; the algorithmic blow-up (3.1–3.3) is 4–6 orders of magnitude.

**Summary.** Three of the four causes are *structural*, not
implementation-level. Porting an algorithm whose iso-equivariance
proof relies on a fixed source vertex into a setting that has no
fixed source vertex destroys the proof and forces a brute-force
n-fold multiplier, which then multiplies against the V-branch
factorial. Optimising the existing greedy_min family cannot recover
more than a constant factor.

## 4. What the hypergraph-iso literature actually uses

The Levi reduction + nauty / Traces / bliss pipeline is not the only
known path. A literature pass (see `docs/research/` and §7) identifies
six relevant strands. The salient finding for IsalHG is:

**4.1 Direct individualisation–refinement (I–R) on hypergraphs.**
Schweitzer & Wiebking (STOC 2019) [SW19] give the only published
canonical-labeling framework that operates natively on hypergraphs
(via a hereditarily-finite-set encoding) and matches the theoretical
complexity of Levi + nauty. **There is no open-source implementation
of this algorithm.** This is the algorithmic family the IsalSR-inspired
encoder should have been compared against; it is the natural target
for an IsalHG-flavoured rewrite that keeps a canonical-string output
but borrows the I–R search discipline.

**4.2 Hypergraph WL.** Böker (WG 2019) [B19] characterises exactly
what 1-WL distinguishes on hypergraphs (homomorphism counts of
Berge-acyclic patterns); this is strictly weaker than nauty's
canonical labelling. Feng et al. (TPAMI 2024) [F24] ship HIC, the
only production-quality native hypergraph fingerprint tool; it is
**incomplete** (collides on the same designs that IsalHG struggles
with). Zhang et al. (ICML 2025) [Z25] introduce k-GWL and show that
distinguishing vertex-transitive designs requires
k ≥ automorphism-orbit-size. The WL-pruning variants we shipped
(`greedy_min_wl_pruned`, `greedy_min_inplace_wl_pruned`) sit inside
this family and inherit its theoretical ceiling.

**4.3 Group-theoretic exact methods.** Luks (STOC 1999) [L99],
Babai-Codenotti (FOCS 2008) [BC08], Neuen (TALG 2022) [N22] give
sub-exponential algorithms for bounded-rank hypergraph iso. None has
a production implementation. Their constants are large; practical
crossover with nauty is unknown.

**4.4 Design-theoretic practice.** Kaski-Östergård [KO04] classified
all STS(19) by combining nauty with a *Pasch-configuration vertex
invariant*. The lesson is the inverse of what IsalHG attempts: do
not try to compete with nauty's canonical labelling; build a strong
domain-specific *pre-partitioning invariant* and pass it to nauty.
GAP's DESIGN package, SageMath's `IncidenceStructure`, and the
HiQGraph database all route through nauty (via GRAPE) for the
canonical-form step. **No community-adopted native hypergraph
canonical-form tool exists below the level of nauty-backed Levi
reduction** — this is the open ecosystem niche IsalHG can fill,
provided the algorithm is rebuilt.

## 5. The actual gap

The preprint sweep was not designed to test "is the IsalHG
canonical-string idea competitive". It was designed to *characterise*
where it wins and loses. The honest answer the data returns is: the
current implementation has no winning regime. The negative result is
publishable in its own right (the preprint's framing in §1 of
`docs/PREPRINT.md` allows for this), but the path forward is not a
larger sweep — it is a different algorithm.

What we want, in priority order, for the full empirical paper:

1. **A native I–R encoder that emits a canonical string.** Adopt the
   Schweitzer-Wiebking [SW19] HFS framework: incidence-tensor
   refinement → individualisation tree → canonical leaf → string
   serialisation. This is the family that gives the only theoretical
   guarantee competitive with Levi + nauty, and is the only path
   that preserves the IsalHG headline ("native canonical string")
   without paying the (r!)^E factor.
2. **A hypergraph-specific pre-partitioning invariant**, computed
   once per seed selection. Direct port of the Kaski-Östergård
   Pasch-configuration tactic [KO04]: count occurrences of the
   smallest forbidden hyperedge configuration (Pasch quad in STS,
   generalised quadrangle line pencil in GQ) per vertex; partition
   seeds by that count *before* the structural-tuple cascade. This
   collapses ξ-equivalence classes by 1–2 orders of magnitude on
   vertex-transitive designs without any change to the canonical
   algorithm.
3. **Drop the multi-seed lex-min idiom entirely.** The IsalSR
   single-source greedy_min is sound only when a distinguished
   source exists. For hypergraphs, replace it with the I–R search
   discipline of (1). The current `greedy_min` should be retired,
   not tuned.
4. **Move the encoder out of pure Python.** Even after (1)–(3),
   parity with nauty (μs/instance) requires a compiled hot loop.
   The IsalSR codebase shows the ceiling pure-Python plus unchecked
   accessors reaches; nauty operates 4–5 orders of magnitude below
   that. A Rust/C extension for the inner refinement loop is the
   minimum implementation tier needed to publish wall-clock numbers
   in the same table as pynauty.

## 6. Concrete recommendations

For the preprint deliverable (already-spent budget):

- **Honestly report the negative result.** The preprint's stated
  framing already allows for "IsalHG dominates in regime X, loses
  in regime Y" outcomes. The current finding is "IsalHG loses in
  every regime tested"; that is the result. Reframe Figure 1 (the
  characterisation heat-grid) to show the DNF map; show the head-
  to-head table from §2.2 as Figure 2; cite [SW19, F24, KO04] in
  §5 as the families that would compete.
- **Withdraw the `greedy_single`/`greedy_min*` family from the
  competitive claim**. Keep them as reference implementations of
  the IsalSR-inspired baseline that motivated the project; do not
  benchmark them against pynauty as if they were equals.
- **Add `feng2024_hic` as a fifth backend.** Feng et al.'s HIC
  (`github.com/iMoonLab/HIC`) is the only direct competitor in the
  "native hypergraph canonical fingerprint" niche. It is incomplete
  but production-quality; benchmarking against it situates IsalHG
  within the right peer group.

For the full empirical paper (next-phase budget):

- **Implement the I–R encoder** as
  `core/algorithms/individualization_refinement.py`. Targets
  Schweitzer-Wiebking equivalence for completeness; integrates the
  Pasch / GQ invariant as the partition refinement signal.
- **Compile-tier rewrite of the inner refinement loop.** Rust via
  PyO3 or a C extension. Match the implementation tier of
  nauty/bliss before claiming wall-clock parity.
- **Restrict the preprint claim** to "*the canonical-string
  invariant is a complete iso fingerprint in pure stdlib Python at
  competitive size*" — fingerprint *size* (bytes per instance) is a
  metric where IsalHG can plausibly win (compact alphabet,
  prefix-friendly), and one we already capture in `fp_bytes_length`.
  Wall-clock is the wrong axis to claim victory on until (1)–(2)
  land.

The seed proposal's contribution claim (*"In which regime of (n, p,
r) does IsalHG outperform Levi + nauty?"*) is unsustainable as a
preprint headline given the current data. The honest reformulation
is the one PI gave in 2026-06-17: *characterise where the native
encoding sits in the algorithmic landscape*. The data says it sits
4–7 orders of magnitude below the Levi baselines. Saying so is the
preprint.
